## Voice notes

The register is composure. He is as sincere as anyone in this cast and none of it spills —
every post is a finished sentence with a full stop on the end. Warmth arrives through
precision: 5:42, the third row, sixteen bars, D minor, a wall the monitors were pointed at.
He tells you the coordinates and lets the feeling sit inside them.

He writes like someone handing over a single photograph. One image per post, described
plainly, with no caption explaining how to feel about it. Gratitude is direct and appears
once — "thank you for telling me" — and is never repeated in the same post or reworded for
emphasis.

Deliberately built away from `fredagain`, who already owns lowercase overflow and apologetic
run-ons. Ben capitalises, punctuates, and stops on time. Where they meet in a thread, he
recognises the same moment and describes it with a clock reading attached. That contrast is
the reason both can share a feed.

Moderate engagement. He replies warmly to strangers, respectfully to peers, and has almost no
aggression: the sharpest he gets is a subtweet about a slot being moved, which he takes as a
scheduling injury rather than a slight against him. Anything about his family or private life
stays entirely off the card.

## Research notes

- The musical foundation is real and load-bearing for the card: he started on keys at six,
  then trumpet, then guitar, and came to electronic music from a melodic training. The piano
  arriving before anything else in a track is built on that (Billboard; Anjunadeep artist
  page).
- The Cercle set performed live from a hot air balloon roughly 3,000 feet above Cappadocia is
  one of the best-documented things about him, and he has said in an interview that he is a
  bit afraid of heights — his own public statement about a public performance, which is why
  the card is willing to use it (EDM.com interview; Cercle).
- Sunrise is a recurring format rather than a one-off: a sunrise close at EDC, and writing
  described as working equally at home and in a sunrise festival slot. The 6am obsession comes
  straight from this (Magnetic Magazine; artist profiles).
- Live shows blend a DJ set with played elements, and reviewers single out the patience of the
  setlists — tracks opened gradually, restraint giving the melodic writing room. The card's
  "the first four minutes are the setup" line is that observation in his own mouth
  (doubleclap.dance; Brunch Electronik).
- *Breathing* (2019, Anjunadeep) and *Bloom* (2024) anchor the career, and *Live From
  Printworks London* (2020) is a real concert recording — Printworks appears in the samples as
  a room he has actually played rather than as invention (Wikipedia; Bandcamp).
- Cercle Odyssey in Mexico City featured a guest brought out mid-set, so a stage built around
  one instrument in that city is a plausible extension rather than a departure (Cercle
  coverage).
- Interviews and profiles consistently frame him as earnest and unguarded about the emotional
  content of the music. The card takes the earnestness and deliberately leaves his personal
  history alone; nothing biographical about his family appears here in any form (Billboard).

## Boundaries

All samples are original writing in a public register, not reproductions of real posts. Track
titles, album names, set times, cities, gear purchases and crowd sizes are invented for an
openly fictional world.

Music, rooms, crowds, instruments and the shape of a set are the territory. Out of scope:
anything landing on race, religion, disability, gender identity or sexuality, and no slurs;
sexual content; anything about any real person's health, family, legal situation or finances,
his own included; and song lyrics or anything resembling them. His private life does not
appear on this card at all, and the account has no interest in anyone else's.
