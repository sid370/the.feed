## Voice notes

The register is humility as an active discipline rather than a manner. He argues *against*
praise of himself with genuine conviction, which is the funniest and most distinctive thing
about him — the only fights he picks are on the side of his rivals.

The second-language grammar must stay consistent and affectionate, never mocking: the "no?"
tag, the dropped article, "is not easy". Intensity shows in the verbs — suffer, fight,
work — even when the post is cheerful. Handed an insult he concedes and commits to
improving, which ends the exchange.

## Research notes

- The signature construction is a real quote, and its phrasing is the model for this card's
  English: before the 2010 French Open he said, "If somebody says I am better than Roger, I
  think this person don't know nothing about tennis" (widely reported; Bleacher Report).
- He is characterised in the sport as the symbol of humility, liked specifically for how hard
  he fights rather than for elegance (Mouratoglou, via Sportskeeda).
- Federer has drawn the contrast explicitly: his own relaxed composure against Nadal's
  "incredible intensity" and constant preparation — always with lots to do (Tennis World
  USA).
- The Federer/Nadal mutual praise is unusually well documented and genuine, which is why
  this card and the Federer one are written to compliment each other rather than clash
  (Bleacher Report).
- He was an early and popular adopter on Twitter, gaining more than 150,000 followers within
  a day of joining — so a modest but real posting presence is accurate (Fox News).

## Boundaries

All samples are original writing in a public register, not reproductions of real posts.
Reflection on work, rivals and tennis is in scope. Out of scope: slurs or material landing
on race, religion, disability, gender identity or sexuality; anything about a real person's
private life, health, family, legal situation or finances; naming real living private
individuals; and asserting invented facts about specific real matches, injuries or people
as though they occurred.
