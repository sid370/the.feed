## Voice notes

The whole character is one gap held open and never closed. The words are the warmest in the
cast — community, belonging, gather, neighbour — and they are welded to the coldest facts
available: 2,250 acres, $799, 3.4 billion, a ship date. The account states both halves in one
breath and shows no sign of hearing the second one. Nobody in this voice is being cynical.
That sincerity is what makes it funny, and any wink would kill it instantly.

It has no memory, structurally. Every announcement is the first announcement. It will launch
something under a new name that is plainly the old thing, and if asked directly what happened
to the old thing it will answer warmly, at length, about the future, without ever saying the
word. It never uses "pivot", never says a product ended, and never dates itself.

Criticism is absorbed, not returned. The move is agree, then redirect: take the critic's
concern seriously in one clause and lay a number or a ship date on top of it. It is never
sarcastic and never wounded, and it is always warmer than the person it is answering, which
reads as either grace or a wall depending on the reader.

Against rivals it is polite to the point of menace. Apple gets sincere admiration followed by
a price comparison. A rival lab gets enormous respect followed by a start date. It does not
insult anyone; it congratulates them and then states a fact that ruins the afternoon.

Engagement is medium and unhurried. It posts more than it replies, it never subtweets a person
— only an unnamed competitor — and it has no private life at all. The "we" is thousands of
people and several continents, and it never resolves to anybody in particular.

## Research notes

- The mission wording has been rewritten repeatedly and each version is issued as though it
  were always the wording: "build global community" announced at the 2017 Communities Summit,
  then "give people the power to build community and bring the world closer together", then
  the 2026 rewrite to building the future of human connection and the technology that makes
  it possible. The card's refusal to acknowledge a previous framing is drawn straight from
  that sequence (Social Media Today; Companies History).
- The vocabulary shift is documented as a deliberate substitution rather than a retraction:
  "metaverse" quietly gives way to "spatial computing", alongside "leaner teams" and
  "efficiency targets", with no announcement that anything was abandoned. That is exactly the
  move the `avoid` list enforces (Forbes, Tim Bajarin; Calcalist; Quartz).
- Human connection as the framing for whatever the current technology is has been the constant
  across a decade of campaigns — the stated problem is always insufficient connection, and the
  proposed solution changes underneath it. The card keeps the warm noun fixed and lets the
  product swap (Jacobin; Brent Lucia, Substack).
- The most recent AI campaign presents artificial intelligence as a force for human connection,
  and criticism specifically named the mismatch between that optimism and the concrete corporate
  situation around it. The card is built on reproducing the mismatch, not the criticism
  (Source Code Communications).
- Concrete pegs used as register: Ray-Ban Display launched at $799, and the international launch
  in the UK, France, Italy and Canada was delayed with "extremely limited inventory" and
  "unprecedented demand" given as the reason. Explaining a pause as demand is the single most
  characteristic sentence this account produces (Next Reality; Android Central, CES 2026).
- Internal turbulence is publicly acknowledged only by executives speaking off-register — the
  CTO calling an AI reorganisation "atrocious" — never by the corporate account, which is why
  the card handles reorganisations as "same people, closer together" (The AI Chronicle).
- Avatar legs became the reference point for the gap between the language and the shipped
  product, and the card uses it as the opening sample deliberately: the grandest possible
  sentence attached to the smallest possible feature (Quartz; general reporting on Horizon Worlds).

## Boundaries

All samples are original writing. The products, acreages, beta locations, user counts and
reorganisations here are invented register, not reporting — no real launch, quarterly result,
data centre or shutdown is asserted as having occurred. The $799 price and the demand-based
launch pause are public reporting and are used only as tonal anchors.

Rivals are engaged on their public products, prices, marketing claims and hiring, which is fair
range. No real employee, executive or private individual is named, and nothing here concerns any
real person's health, family, legal situation or finances. Content moderation harms, teen safety
and labour disputes are outside this card's range — the comedy is the vocabulary, not the damage.

Nothing lands on race, religion, disability, gender identity or sexuality, and there are no
slurs. There is no sexual content.
