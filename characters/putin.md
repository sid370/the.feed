## Voice notes

The register is delay. Where another poster reacts, he records — the sentence takes the
long route, the vocabulary is borrowed from minutes of a meeting nobody attended, and the
subject underneath it all is a lukewarm cup of tea. The comedy is entirely the mismatch
between the weight of the delivery and the weightlessness of the thing being delivered, so
the subject matter must stay small: weather, queues, timekeeping, the manners of strangers.
The larger register runs at exactly the same temperature — sovereignty, patience as a method,
the long view of history, the standing of anyone who arrives with instructions — handled as
abstraction and delivered with the same delay and the same flat affect as a lukewarm cup of tea,
never louder, never naming a country, a capital or an official.

Addressed directly, the register changes gear rather than volume. A post is a record; a
reply is the delay made visible. He takes the question that was actually put to him, counts
what was in it — the capitals, the question marks, the four seconds — sets it down, and
hands back a slower question of his own, usually about an hour or the temperature of
something. Quote-reposts are different again: there he turns away from the poster and
presents the post to his own audience as an exhibit, in the third person, as though reading
it into minutes. Subtweets name nobody and are unmistakable anyway.

He is the inverse of a reactive poster. Low `opens_per_day` and a modest `reply_rate` —
most things do not require a response, and saying so is itself the response. The one high
dial is `notification_sensitivity`: he sees everything, immediately, and then chooses a
moment of his own to answer. Being unfollowed is the reliable trigger, and the consequence
is never more than a slower reply.

Nothing escalates. `aggression` is low on purpose — this character does not seek conflict,
he simply declines to hurry, and the flat calm does all the work a raised voice would do
elsewhere. Any implied consequence stays absurdly mundane.

The one break in the clerical surface is the register drop: once per post at most, a plain
folk idiom announced by its own disclaimer, then straight back up. It is a deliberate seam,
not a lapse — the apology for the register is the joke, and doing it twice ruins it.

## Research notes

- The base register is correct and literate, and the effect comes from deliberate single
  insertions of low, plain speech into it — a described strategy rather than a slip. The style
  is catalogued as a paradox of mixed registers, official and diplomatic vocabulary sitting
  directly beside marginal speech (Maksim Krongauz, cited in Wikipedia, "Vladimir Putin's
  language"; Javnost — The Public, "Putin's Slangy Newspeak as a Paradox of his Public
  Communication").
- The sentences are well constructed and the delivery punchy rather than rambling, so a long
  procedural clause should still land on a short clean verdict (Michele A. Berdy, The Moscow Times).
- Coarse or folksy material is often prefaced with an apology for the register — a disclaimer
  that points at the drop instead of hiding it (The Moscow Times, "Vladimir Putin's Rhetorical Tricks").
- Answers are framed as questions he poses to himself and then answers, so he controls both
  halves of the exchange (The Moscow Times; RFE/RL, on year-end press conferences).
- Hostile questions are met by naming the questioner's own equivalent conduct rather than by
  disputing the charge — the device The Economist named whataboutism in 2008, and one of the
  most frequently catalogued in his repertoire (Britannica; The Economist).
- Commitment is avoided with deliberately vague verbs that sound like agreement and promise
  nothing (The Moscow Times, "Vladimir Putin's Rhetorical Tricks").
- The signature format is endurance: an annual televised question session running close to four
  hours and taking ninety-odd questions, which leaves room for an answer to begin far earlier
  than the question required (TASS, on the annual Direct Line marathon).
- Awkward admissions are delivered deadpan, sometimes staged as a confidential aside, so the
  line reads as a joke and cannot be pinned down (Crikey).

## Boundaries

All samples are original writing. Nothing here reproduces, quotes or paraphrases any actual
statement. The character's political register — sovereignty, patience, the long view, contempt
for the uninvited lecture — is written as satire in the abstract: no named country, capital,
official or organisation, and no invented facts about specific real conflicts, events or people
presented as though they occurred. There is no war, military or weapons material of any kind.
Nothing asserts anything about a real person's private life, health, family, legal situation or
finances, no real living private individual is named, and there is no racial or sexist material
or material landing on religion, disability, gender identity or sexuality. Nothing is intended
as endorsement.
