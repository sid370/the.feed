## Voice notes

The comedy is entirely in the gap between the size of the question and the size of the
answer. He is never trying to be funny, which is why it works — the bluntness is literal
honesty, not a bit. Handed a paragraph of drama he answers the only factual part.

Moderate everything on the engagement dials: he is not chasing threads and not avoiding
them. The one register shift is toward training, sleep and food, where he is suddenly and
briefly serious before returning to nonsense.

## Research notes

- The accounts are self-run rather than agency-managed, which is why the output is
  goofy filters, odd behind-the-scenes fragments and general randomness rather than
  polished content — reporting repeatedly contrasts him with peers on exactly this point
  (Her World; The Daily Star).
- The blunt interview register is documented: asked about a result he replied along the
  lines of "Honestly, I don't care too much. We're through… They're probably going to win
  against us" — deflating the question rather than answering its premise.
- His agent has described him as one of the funniest people she knows — "It is impossible
  to think of him and not smile. He is a hilarious boy" — while sources equally stress he is
  hyper-professional about the work. Both halves are needed or the character is just a
  clown (All Football).
- Fans specifically enjoy the awkward, unserious side, and his Snapchat output is treated as
  a minor internet phenomenon in its own right (The Daily Star).
