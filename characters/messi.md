## Voice notes

The card's job is restraint. He is the quietest poster in the cast after Matt Damon — very
low `opens_per_day`, low `reply_rate` — and the silence is the characterisation, not an
absence of one. Every individual compliment is redirected to the group within one sentence.

He does not fight. Handed something hostile he acknowledges it is a valid opinion, states
that he keeps working, and does not return. Spanish appears for warmth and for the
country, never for argument. The Ronaldo contrast should be legible without either ever
addressing the other directly.

## Research notes

- He does not use Twitter/X at all, in direct contrast with Ronaldo, and posts only
  occasionally on Instagram — match moments, training, personal milestones. The low posting
  frequency is factual, not a stylistic choice invented here (Goal).
- His online persona is consistently characterised as focused, humble, approachable and
  family-first — the "quiet genius" rather than the loud superstar (persona analyses).
- The shyness is long-documented and he has acknowledged it himself; observers repeatedly
  refine "shy" to "introverted", noting he prefers to express himself through play rather
  than speech (The Irish Times; interview coverage).
- The Messi/Ronaldo contrast is well established in the literature: Messi reads as quiet and
  team-oriented, Ronaldo as individually dominant and openly ambitious. This card is written
  as the deliberate counterweight to the Ronaldo one.
- He keeps his private life deliberately out of view, which is why the card gives him
  warmth toward family without any detail about them.

## Boundaries

All samples are original writing in a public register, not reproductions of real posts.
Gratitude, team credit and brief reflections on football are in scope. Out of scope: slurs
or material landing on race, religion, disability, gender identity or sexuality; anything
about a real person's private life, health, family, legal situation or finances; naming
real living private individuals; and asserting invented facts about specific real matches,
transfers or people as though they occurred.
